# AEO — getting cited by AI answer engines

Companion to `knowledge.md`. This is the fastest-moving and most folklore-ridden
area in SEO, so the labels matter:

- **[P-verified]** — primary source, fetched and read in this repo's refresh.
- **[P]** — primary source, reported by the research pass but not re-read by hand.
- **[S]** — secondary (vendor study, trade press). Useful, not authoritative.
- **[unverified]** — circulating claim with no credible source found. Do not act on it.

Last full refresh: **2026-10-03**.

---

## 1. What Google says to do (and explicitly not to do)

Google publishes a dedicated guide: **"Optimizing your website for generative AI
features on Google Search"**
(https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).
Direct quotes, all **[P-verified]** 2026-10-03:

| Popular tactic | Google's words |
|---|---|
| llms.txt / AI files / Markdown | "You don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, markup, or Markdown to appear in Google Search… neither harm nor help your site's visibility." |
| Chunking content for AI | "There's no requirement to break your content into tiny pieces for AI to better understand it." |
| Writing for AI / keyword rewrites | "You don't need to write in a specific way just for generative AI search. AI systems can understand synonyms and general meanings." |
| Chasing brand mentions | "Seeking inauthentic 'mentions' across the web isn't as helpful as it might seem." |
| Schema for AI | "Structured data isn't required for generative AI search, and there's no special schema.org markup you need to add." |

**Query fan-out is confirmed mechanics**: AI Mode/AI Overviews issue "a set of
concurrent, related queries generated by the model… to fetch additional relevant
search results". So a page can be pulled into an answer for a question the user
never typed — which is the real argument for covering a topic thoroughly rather
than one keyword at a time. **[P-verified]**

What Google actually recommends: unique non-commodity content with first-hand
perspective, clear headers and sections, semantic HTML, supporting images/video,
few duplicates, good page experience, structured data that matches visible text
(optional), and monitoring in Search Console. **[P-verified]**

## 2. What the research literature supports

- **Extractable evidence helps.** The original GEO paper measured, on documents already in the model's context: quotations +41% position-adjusted word count, statistics +31%, cited sources ~+40%. — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.09735 **[P]**
- **Read the caveat before quoting that 40%.** The 2026 survey shows it is a *relative* lift measured only on documents already injected into a 5-document context. It says nothing about organic discoverability or traffic. — https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14035v1 (2026-07-15) **[P]**
- **Most reproducible factors:** topical relevance, and position in the retrieved context. Retrieval rank dominates on-page rewriting — moving a source higher in context beats any textual tweak. **[P]** same survey
- **What does not replicate:** keyword stuffing (null to negative), fixed formatting recipes across engines, fluency optimisation. C-SEO Bench found only **3 of 54** method×domain combinations significantly positive. **[P]** same survey
- **No technique has a stable, longitudinal, cross-platform causal effect on organic discoverability.** As more publishers adopt the same heuristics, individual gains erode toward zero. **[P]** same survey
- **Citation ≠ traffic, and citation ≠ accuracy.** Only ~51.5% of sentences in cited sources are fully supported by them, and citation doesn't predict clicks. **[P]** same survey

## 3. Measured context (useful for expectations, not instructions)

- AI Overviews appear on 13.7% of all queries but **64.7% of question-phrased** searches. This measures whether an AI answer *appears*, not whether you're cited — "question-shaped H2s get you cited" is **[unverified]**. **[P]** survey
- 76.1% of AI-Overview-cited pages also rank in the organic top 10; **14.4% don't rank at all**; median organic position of the top citation is 2 (n=1.9M citations). — https://ahrefs.com/blog/search-rankings-ai-citations/ **[S]** 2025-07-21
- Cross-engine overlap is low (Jaccard 0.11–0.18 between Google organic, AIO and Gemini), so ranking and AI citation are related but distinct games. **[P]** survey
- **Freshness: the update date matters more than the publish date.** Of 47,097 citations, 75% of cited pages were updated within a year, 88% within two; by publication date only 42% are recent. — https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/study-content-recencys-impact-on-ai-visibility-in-2026 **[S]** 2026-07
  - But Google names **changing dates without substantially updating content** as a quality red flag **[P-verified]**. Update genuinely, then change the date.
- **Word count barely correlates** with AIO citation (Spearman ≈0.04) — long posts for their own sake buy nothing. **[S]** Ahrefs 2026-01-20
- Highest correlations Ahrefs found with AI-Overview brand visibility: **YouTube mentions 0.737**, branded web mentions 0.664, backlinks only 0.218 — all explicitly correlation, not causation. **[S]** (2025-05-26 study, often mis-dated 2026)
  - Relevant to us: Teja's YouTube presence is the highest-correlated signal in that dataset, and it's one we already own. Cross-linking channel ↔ blog is cheap.
- Most-cited AIO domains are platforms: YouTube 22.9%, Reddit 18.5%, Facebook 10.1%. **[S]** Ahrefs 2026-09. Being present where answers get sourced matters as much as your own pages.
- Click reality: −34.5% CTR with AIO present (Ahrefs); Pew found users click a traditional result on 8% of visits with an AI summary vs 15% without. **[S]**

## 4. llms.txt — the honest status

- **No major AI company reads it in production.** Google documents it as unnecessary and ignored; it appears nowhere in OpenAI's, Anthropic's or Perplexity's bot docs. **[P-verified]** (Google), **[P]** (others)
- Measured: of 137,210 domains, 28% publish an llms.txt and **97% of those files got zero requests** in a month; crawlers never even probe for it. — https://ahrefs.com/blog/llmstxt-study/ **[S]** 2026-05
- It is **not** an IETF standard. The real standards work is the IETF **aipref** WG (`draft-ietf-aipref-vocab`, `draft-ietf-aipref-attach`, which updates RFC 9309), separating **Search / Agent / Training** uses. **[P]**
- Cloudflare's **Content Signals Policy** (`search`, `ai-input`, `ai-train` in robots.txt) is the deployed convention, with no enforcement. **[P]**
- **Verdict: don't build one.** Revisit if aipref ships and engines announce support.

## 5. Crawlers — who to allow to stay citable

Vendor docs, checked 2026-10-03. **Crawling for answers and crawling for
training are different bots**; this is the table that actually changes decisions.

| Engine | Allow for citation | Training-only (safe to block) | Live user fetch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google | `Googlebot` (also serves AI Overviews / AI Mode — there is no separate AI crawler) | `Google-Extended` (Gemini training/grounding; blocking does **not** remove you from AI Overviews) | — |
| Bing / Copilot | `bingbot` (serves both) | — | — |
| ChatGPT | `OAI-SearchBot` | `GPTBot` | `ChatGPT-User` (robots.txt "may not apply") |
| Claude | `Claude-SearchBot` | `ClaudeBot` | `Claude-User` (honors robots.txt) |
| Perplexity | `PerplexityBot` | — (not used for training) | `Perplexity-User` ("generally ignores robots.txt") |

**Minimum allow-list:** `Googlebot`, `bingbot`, `OAI-SearchBot`, `Claude-SearchBot`, `PerplexityBot`.
Our `robots.ts` files currently allow everything except `/dashboard`, which satisfies this. Don't "optimise" it.

Context: AI training crawlers went from 22% → **52% of all crawler requests**
between spring 2025 and June 2026; >50% of internet traffic is now non-human.
— https://blog.cloudflare.com/agentic-web/ **[P]** 2026-09-30

## 6. Measurement available today

- **Google Search Console → Generative AI performance report.** Covers AI Overviews + AI Mode. **Impressions only — no clicks, no CTR, no position, no queries.** Breakdowns: page, country, device, date, search type. Rolled out to all sites worldwide as of **2026-08-31**. — https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139 **[P-verified]**
  - AI *clicks* are still folded into the main Performance report under search type "Web". That's the live gap. **[P-verified]**
- **Bing Webmaster Tools → AI Performance** (preview) is the only tool giving citation counts *and* the queries behind them: Total Citations, Average Cited Pages, **Grounding Queries**, plus Intents, Topics and Citation Share since 2026-06-16. **[P]** Worth setting up for PFT — Bing is less contested than Google for a new site.
- **GA4 has a native "AI Assistant" channel group** isolating referrals from ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot, Grok. **[S]** 2026-05. Google AI Overviews/AI Mode clicks remain **indistinguishable** from ordinary organic.
- ChatGPT appends `utm_source=chatgpt.com` to cited links — real but **undocumented** by OpenAI, so it can change silently. **[S]**
- Third-party trackers (Ahrefs Brand Radar, Semrush AI Visibility, Profound, Peec, Otterly…) all work by prompt sampling; none have platform data. The survey's methodological bar for meaningful sampling is **7–8 repetitions per prompt** with paraphrase variation — no commercial tracker documents that. Treat their numbers as directional. **[P]**
- Perplexity has a Publishers' Program (revenue share) but **no citation reporting**. Claims about its "3× subscriber weighting" or "entity density multipliers" are **[unverified]** agency speculation.

## 7. What this means for our blogs

1. **Don't build AEO-specific machinery.** Google's own guide rules out llms.txt, chunking, AI-specific markup and writing-for-AI. Anyone charging for those is selling folklore.
2. **Do include extractable evidence** — real statistics with sources, short verbatim quotes from official sources, clear definitions, dates. This is the one technique with experimental support, and it's also just good journalism.
3. **Cover topics, not keywords** — query fan-out means breadth across a cluster gets you pulled into answers you didn't target.
4. **Keep posts updated, honestly.** Update date correlates with citation; faked dates are a quality violation.
5. **Teja's YouTube is an asset** — the highest-correlated signal in the biggest dataset available. Cross-link channel and blog.
6. **Expect fewer clicks per impression** than classic SEO. Judge AI visibility by impressions and brand presence, not only sessions.
7. **Set up Bing Webmaster Tools** — the only place we can see which queries caused a citation.
